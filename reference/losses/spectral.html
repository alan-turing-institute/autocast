Composing grid-space and spectral losses#

ensemble_fourier_crps adds coefficient-wise complex CRPS to grid-space CRPS. It scores complex coefficients, not their power or magnitude alone, so coefficient phase differences contribute to the loss.

Using the preset#

Select the loss in an ensemble EPD configuration. For example, to adapt the existing ensemble ViT experiment to your data:

uv run autocast epd \
    experiment=epd_crps_vit_large_ps4_64 \
    datamodule.data_path=/path/to/data \
    loss@model.loss_func=ensemble_fourier_crps \
    model.train_in_latent_space=false \
    model.n_members=8

Keep the data, encoder, decoder and processor settings appropriate to your dataset. The loss preset overrides an experiment’s loss selection but does not otherwise change its architecture or training settings. Without this selection, existing loss defaults are unchanged.

This EPD preset clears the previous model.loss_func mapping before composing its replacement, so old constructor arguments are not inherited. Keep the @model.loss_func package shown above; explicit command-line overrides of the new loss’s weights and scores are applied afterwards as usual.

The preset requires decoded fields and at least two members. EPD setup rejects it when model.train_in_latent_space=true; the latent training path does not provide the required channels-last field layout. This is distinct from using a Fourier transform on an explicitly arranged latent grid in custom code. Decoded residual prediction (model.residual_prediction=true) is supported: the loss scores reconstructed states, not raw predicted increments.

The equivalent Python objective is:

from autocast.losses import (
    AlphaFairCRPSLoss,
    CoefficientCRPSLoss,
    FourierDecomposition,
    TransformedLoss,
    WeightedSumLoss,
)

loss = WeightedSumLoss(
    losses=[
        AlphaFairCRPSLoss(alpha=0.95),
        TransformedLoss(
            FourierDecomposition(representation="complex", norm="ortho"),
            score=CoefficientCRPSLoss(alpha=0.95),
            requires_ambient_predictions=True,
        ),
    ],
    weights=[1.0, 0.1],
)
# predictions: (B, T, S1, ..., Sd, C, M)
# targets:     (B, T, S1, ..., Sd, C)
# objective = loss(predictions, targets)

Exact scoring convention#

For one real or complex coefficient, with members \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) and target \(y\), CoefficientCRPSLoss computes

\[ S_\alpha = \frac{1}{M}\sum_i |z_i-y| - \left(\frac{1-\alpha}{M^2}+\frac{\alpha}{M(M-1)}\right) \sum_{i<j}|z_i-z_j|. \]

Here \(|\cdot|\) is complex modulus (Euclidean distance in the real-imaginary plane). This is the coefficient-wise complex scoring convention used for spectral CRPS in BriS, Section 2.2. It is not independent scoring of the real and imaginary parts. alpha=0 (the class default) gives the plug-in score, alpha=1 gives the fair score, and intermediate values interpolate. A single member is supported only at alpha=0, returning absolute coefficient error. The preset uses alpha=0.95 for both terms, corresponding to a fairness adjustment of \(0.05/M\).

FourierDecomposition transforms spatial axes only, using the full FFT with orthonormal normalization in this preset, and returns a "fourier" component. It leaves batch, time, channel and member axes untouched. Each frequency and channel is scored independently before reduction. reduction="none" retains the target shape; "mean" (default) and "sum" reduce all its axes. The preset therefore computes mean grid-space score + 0.1 x mean Fourier score, without dividing by 1.1. Full-FFT frequency multiplicities matter; replacing it with an unweighted real FFT mean changes this objective.

The loss uses the units of its inputs, including any data normalization. It does not denormalize them. “Grid-space” is not a promise of physical SI units. Orthonormal FFT does not equalize CRPS magnitudes or gradients, and the weight 0.1 is not a guaranteed 10% gradient contribution. Independent coefficient scores do not determine all cross-frequency dependencies or guarantee physical balance.

The score uses direct modulus, without smoothing, and PyTorch’s zero subgradient at exact coincidence. The Fourier boundary promotes float16 and bfloat16 fields to float32 on the same device; double precision is preserved. Direct coefficient scoring also uses the common input dtype with at least float32 precision (complex64 for complex inputs), before computing distances, to avoid low-precision subtraction or pairwise-sum overflow. The score has the corresponding real dtype; gradients retain each input’s dtype. FFT support still depends on the execution device. Pairwise scoring has quadratic cost in ensemble size; it evaluates only unordered member pairs.

Radial bands#

RadialFourierDecomposition selects disjoint bands of complex Fourier coefficients, named band_0, band_1, and so on. Each component retains the full grid layout with zeros outside its band. It does not average phases or replace coefficients with a radial power spectrum. Band membership depends on grid shape and edges, not on prediction or target precision.

Edges are radial frequencies in cycles per grid cell, not integer Fourier mode numbers or physical wavenumbers. The first band begins at zero; intervals include their lower edge and exclude their upper edge, except that the final upper edge is included. In two dimensions the corners reach approximately 0.7071 cycles/cell, beyond the per-axis Nyquist of 0.5. Use a final edge of at least sqrt(n_spatial_dims) / 2 to cover every mode. A shorter cutoff leaves higher frequencies unscored by this loss. Holding edges fixed across different resolutions preserves grid-relative, not physical, scales.

For example, this two-dimensional objective emphasizes the highest band:

from autocast.losses import (
    AlphaFairCRPSLoss,
    CoefficientCRPSLoss,
    RadialFourierDecomposition,
    TransformedLoss,
    WeightedSumLoss,
)

radial_loss = TransformedLoss(
    RadialFourierDecomposition(edges=[0.1, 0.25, 0.71]),
    score=CoefficientCRPSLoss(alpha=0.95),
    component_weights={"band_0": 1.0, "band_1": 1.0, "band_2": 2.0},
    requires_ambient_predictions=True,
)
loss = WeightedSumLoss([AlphaFairCRPSLoss(alpha=0.95), radial_loss], [1.0, 0.1])

The existing ensemble_fourier_crps preset remains full-spectrum and unchanged. A radial term can also be combined with a full Fourier term through WeightedSumLoss. Memory grows with the number of full-sized band components.

Reduction matters: with its default reduction, coefficient CRPS averages over the full grid, including zeroed positions. TransformedLoss then takes a weighted mean across components. Consequently, an equally weighted, complete partition into J bands with band_mean=False gives the full Fourier score divided by J, not a new cross-frequency constraint. Weights or cutoffs change the emphasis. Empty bands score zero but still count in the component-weight denominator unless excluded or given zero weight.

The optional band_mean=True divides each band’s coefficients by the square root of its mode count, so its summed squared magnitude becomes mean band power. For coefficient CRPS it instead scales the band score by 1 / sqrt(count); it does not compute mean CRPS over just the occupied modes or automatically equalize bands. It is disabled by default.

Composition and extension contracts#

  • WeightedSumLoss sums whole-loss outputs with non-negative weights.

  • FieldDecomposition maps a field to named components. IdentityDecomposition returns "field"; SequentialDecomposition composes stages with slash-separated component names.

  • ComponentBranch selects components, applies shared ComponentTransforms independently to prediction and target, then forms their weighted mean.

  • ComponentObjective takes a weighted sum of ScoringBranch outputs.

  • TransformedLoss combines a decomposition with either a score= convenience argument or an explicit objective=, not both. An explicit objective controls its own component aggregation.

All aggregated score outputs must have the same shape; scalar/array broadcasting is rejected. Transforms and branches receive n_spatial_dims and must preserve the declared field layout. The requires_ambient_predictions flag is optional on TransformedLoss; WeightedSumLoss propagates it from any configured term, including nested and zero-weight terms, which are still evaluated.

An unreduced Fourier score is indexed by frequency, not physical position. Do not apply a physical-cell mask to those bins as though they were spatial errors. The supplied EPD preset returns a scalar and avoids that ambiguity.

class FourierDecomposition(representation='complex', norm='ortho')[source]#

Apply a full ND Fourier transform over channels-last spatial axes.

Inputs follow (B, T, S1, ..., Sd, C) with an optional final ensemble axis. The transform preserves that layout and transforms spatial axes only. It does not transform time, channels or ensemble members.

Half and bfloat16 inputs are promoted to float32 on the same device before the FFT. Single and double precision are preserved, including complex inputs. The cast and transform remain differentiable.

Parameters:

  • representation (Literal['complex', 'magnitude', 'power']) – Return complex coefficients, their magnitudes or their squared magnitudes, under the component name fourier.

  • norm (Literal['backward', 'forward', 'ortho']) – FFT normalization. ortho scales coefficients by the reciprocal square root of the number of spatial cells; forward uses the reciprocal cell count and backward applies no scaling.

class RadialFourierDecomposition(edges, norm='ortho', band_mean=False)[source]#

Split complex Fourier coefficients into disjoint radial bands.

Each band_<index> retains the full field layout, with coefficients outside that band set to zero. This selects coefficients, not a radial average of their power. Frequencies beyond the final edge are omitted. Spatial axes, FFT normalization and precision follow FourierDecomposition.

band_mean=True divides coefficients by the square root of the number of modes in their band. Summing their squared magnitudes then yields mean band power. This does not give a within-band mean for coefficient CRPS: its reduction still includes zeroed positions across the full grid. Band weights and the scoring objective determine the final aggregation. Empty bands return zero tensors. Memory grows with the number of bands.

Parameters:

  • edges (Sequence[int | float]) – Finite, positive, strictly increasing upper edges in cycles per grid cell. The first band starts at zero. Lower edges are included; upper edges are excluded except for the final edge, which is included. A final edge of at least sqrt(n_spatial_dims) / 2 covers the entire grid, including diagonal Nyquist modes. These are grid-relative frequencies, not physical wavenumbers.

  • norm (Literal['backward', 'forward', 'ortho']) – FFT normalization, as in FourierDecomposition.

  • band_mean (bool) – Divide each nonempty band’s coefficients by sqrt(mode count).
