class LogScore(var_floor=1e-08, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: BTSCMMetric

Per-site logarithmic score (marginal Gaussian ignorance) for ensembles.

For each grid point, fit a Gaussian to the \(M\) ensemble members using their sample mean \(\mu\) and Bessel-corrected sample variance \(\sigma^2\), then return the Gaussian negative log-density at the observation \(y\):

\[\mathrm{LogS}(y) = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\!\bigl(2\pi\sigma^2\bigr) + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{(y - \mu)^2}{\sigma^2}.\]

This is the marginal (per-site, diagonal-covariance) logarithmic score – the univariate companion to the multivariate DawidSebastianiScore, which reintroduces the cross-site covariance. It is a local strictly-proper scoring rule: unlike CRPS (which rewards marginal-CDF calibration) it is driven by the predictive density value at the realised truth, so it penalises a too-narrow predictive that misses a tail observation especially sharply – the reason it anchors the tail read of column 1 of the assessment suite. Being moment-based it can be evaluated identically for sample-only ensembles (flow matching, CRPS-trained) and for models with closed-form predictive densities.

A hard var_floor guards the near-deterministic regime where the members collapse and \(\sigma^2 \to 0\). The additive \(\tfrac12\log 2\pi\) is retained so the score equals the Gaussian negative log-density exactly.

References: Good, I.J. (1952). Rational decisions. J. R. Stat. Soc. B, 14(1), 107-114. Gneiting, T. and Raftery, A.E. (2007). Strictly proper scoring rules, prediction, and estimation. J. Amer. Statist. Assoc., 102(477), 359-378.

Parameters:

var_floor (float)

class AlmostFairEnergyScore(a_f=0.95, vector_dims='spatial_temporal_channels', **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: BTSCMMetric

Almost-fair joint-field energy score (multivariate afCRPS, beta=1).

The validation-time mirror of the DriftingProcessor energy-score loss term (research piece sec. 3.2): the mean Euclidean distance of ensemble members to the truth minus an almost-fair spread reward over distinct member pairs, with the distance taken over the joint (vectorised) field.

\[\mathrm{afES} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_m \lVert x_m - y\rVert_2 - \frac{1-\varepsilon}{2M(M-1)} \sum_{m}\sum_{j} \lVert x_m - x_j\rVert_2,\]

with \(\varepsilon = (1 - a_f)/M\). The diagonal terms vanish (||x_m - x_m|| = 0), so the double sum equals the distinct-pair sum. The coefficient recovers the fair estimator at a_f = 1 and the naive EnergyScore (1/(2M^2)) at a_f -> 0.

This is the same almost-fair convex blend as AlphaFairCRPS, but the coefficient carries the factor of 2 over distinct ordered pairs that the 1-D order-statistic CRPS reduction omits (different objects, do not reconcile).

References: Lang et al. (2024), AIFS-CRPS, arXiv:2412.15832.

class TemporalAutocorrelationMatch(max_lag=None, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: BTSCMMetric

Match between ensemble and observed temporal autocorrelation (per site).

A lead-axis diagnostic for column 3 (temporal dependence): does the ensemble reproduce the observed across-lead autocorrelation structure of each series? For every (batch, site, channel) it forms the member-pooled sample autocorrelation of the ensemble trajectories over the lead axis and the autocorrelation of the observed trajectory, then reports the mean over lags 1..K of the absolute per-lag discrepancy

\[\frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \big| r_k^{\text{ens}} - r_k^{\text{obs}} \big|,\]

which grows as the sampled trajectories become too smooth (over-correlated) or too rough (under-correlated). max_lag (K) defaults to T - 1 (all available lags); higher lags carry fewer samples and are noisier.

Finite-``T`` floor (read carefully). The value approaches 0 for a matched ensemble only as T -> inf. Because r_k^{obs} is estimated from a single length-T observed trajectory it is noisy, so |r_k^{ens} - r_k^{obs}| is biased strictly positive even for a perfectly matched ensemble – empirically the mean discrepancy for a matched AR(1) ensemble is ~0.4 at T = 8, ~0.13 at T = 32, ~0.03 at T = 256. At the short lead windows typical of rollout eval the metric therefore reports a nonzero floor for a matched ensemble; read it against the matched-ensemble null (the Phase-4 battery calibrates the threshold this way), never against a literal 0.

This is an interpretable diagnostic, NOT a proper scoring rule – it can be gamed (an ensemble need not be calibrated to match a marginal autocorrelation) and must not drive model selection on its own. It complements, never replaces, the temporal variogram (variogram_temporal): the variogram gives a proper-score magnitude of the temporal-dependence error, this gives its lag-resolved shape.

References: Pinson, P., & Girard, R. (2012). Evaluating the quality of scenarios of short-term wind power generation. Applied Energy, 96, 12-20.

class DawidSebastianiScore(vector_dims='spatial', shrinkage='ledoit_wolf', shrinkage_intensity=None, jitter=1e-06, max_pair_elements=134217728, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: BTSCMMetric

Multivariate Dawid-Sebastiani score (full-covariance Gaussian ignorance).

Over the vector selected by vector_dims (default "spatial": the per-channel spatial field), fit a Gaussian to the \(M\) ensemble members using their mean vector \(\mu\) and a regularised covariance \(\Sigma\), then return the multivariate Gaussian negative log-density at the observation \(y \in \mathbb{R}^D\):

\[\mathrm{DSS}(y) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(D\log 2\pi + \log\det\Sigma + (y - \mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (y - \mu)\bigr).\]

Because \(\Sigma^{-1}\) couples the components, this is sensitive to the forecast’s correlation structure, not only its marginals – the property that makes it the column-2 dependence complement to the marginal LogScore. It is the parametric-Gaussian counterpart to the nonparametric VariogramScore; the two rest on different assumptions, so they catch different correlation errors.

Covariance regularisation (the load-bearing choice)#

With \(M\) members and \(D\) sites the raw sample covariance has rank \(\le M - 1\), so it is singular (and \(\Sigma^{-1}\) undefined) whenever \(D > M - 1\) – the usual case. The covariance is therefore shrunk toward a scaled identity, \(\Sigma = (1-\gamma) S + \gamma\,\nu I + \varepsilon I\). By default \(\gamma\) is the data-driven Ledoit-Wolf intensity (shrinkage= "ledoit_wolf"); shrinkage="fixed" uses a caller-supplied shrinkage_intensity. Shrinkage pulls \(\Sigma\) toward independence, so an over-aggressive \(\gamma\) damps the very correlation-sensitivity this score exists to measure – the Ledoit-Wolf default keeps it minimal and adaptive, and the assessment suite’s planted-correlation battery is the guardrail that the active regularisation has not flattened the signal.

This is a proper scoring rule; it is strictly proper only within the Gaussian family, so it is reported as a dependence complement, never the sole dependence judge. Computed in float64. Since 2026-08-19 the covariance is never materialised: it is diagonal-plus-rank-\(M\), so _shrunk_gaussian_terms() takes both required quantities from an \(M \times M\) factorisation, making the metric \(O(D M^2 + M^3)\) in time and linear in \(D\) in memory. memory_intensive is nevertheless left True: flipping it would change which passes the eval gate admits this metric to, which is a scoring decision rather than a performance one, and belongs to whoever owns the metric set.

References: Dawid, A.P. and Sebastiani, P. (1999). Coherent dispersion criteria for optimal experimental design. Ann. Statist., 27(1), 65-81. Ledoit, O. and Wolf, M. (2004). A well-conditioned estimator for large-dimensional covariance matrices. J. Multivar. Anal., 88(2), 365-411.

torch.cdist compute mode, PINNED – never leave this to the default.

⛔ The default (use_mm_for_euclid_dist_if_necessary) switches to a ||x||^2 + ||y||^2 - 2 x.y expansion once either input has more than 25 rows, and that form cancels catastrophically for near-coincident points – which is precisely a collapsing ensemble, the regime the spread term exists to police. Measured on this repo’s shapes (float32, D=16384, near-identical members): at M=8 both modes agree with a float64 reference to 1.0e-07, but at M=32 the default is 1.775 relative error – 177% wrong – against 1.2e-07 pinned. class PooledSpreadSkillRatio(**kwargs)[source]#

Bases: BTSCMMetric

Spread-to-skill ratio with the division taken once, after all averaging.

Same quantity as SpreadSkillRatio, differing only in where the division sits relative to the averaging over forecast cases. SpreadSkillRatio forms one ratio per case (after reducing over the spatial axes) and averages those ratios; this class accumulates the variance and the squared error separately over cases and space, and divides once:

\[\mathrm{SSR}_{\text{pooled}} = \sqrt{ \frac{\sum \mathrm{Var}_{m,\text{unbiased}}(x_m)} {\sum (\bar{x} - y)^2}} \; \sqrt{\frac{M + 1}{M}} .\]

The distinction is not cosmetic. The calibration identity behind the metric – \(\mathrm{E}[\mathrm{Var}] = \sigma^2\) and \(\mathrm{E}[(\bar{x} - y)^2] = \sigma^2 (M+1)/M\), so the corrected ratio is 1 for a calibrated ensemble – constrains the ratio of the expectations, not the expectation of the ratio. A per-case denominator is a noisy estimate of the typical squared error, and because it sits under the division, cases where the truth happens to land near the ensemble mean push their ratio up without bound while unlucky cases can only push it down toward zero. Averaging those ratios therefore lands high, by an amount set by how many effectively independent values each case contributes – i.e. by the field’s spatial correlation length, not by its grid size. On a provably calibrated ensemble the per-case form reads 1.11 on a 32-site correlated ring and up to 2.05 on a smooth 16x16 field, while this class reads 1 to within sampling noise in both.

Notes

Reported per lead time and channel: the sums reduce over the batch and the spatial axes, leaving the lead axis intact, so the output granularity matches SpreadSkillRatio(score_dims="spatial"). Only that reduction is supported – pooling is the whole point of this class, so a score_dims that would keep the batch or spatial axes separate is rejected rather than silently ignored.

Bases: BTSCMMetric

Ensemble spread for probabilistic forecasts.

Note

By default, returns a finite-ensemble corrected spread:

\[\text{Spread}_{\text{corr}} = \sqrt{\left\langle \mathrm{Var}_{m,\text{unbiased}}(x_m)\right\rangle} \sqrt{\frac{M + 1}{M}}.\]

This correction is commonly used so that spread and skill are comparable for finite ensemble sizes when using unbiased sample variance. It matches the form used in LoLA/paper evaluations (Appendix “Spread / Skill”) where: spread = sqrt((M+1)/(M-1) * mean((x_m - mean_m)^2)), since Var_unbiased = (M/(M-1)) * mean((x_m - mean_m)^2).

If corrected=False, returns the uncorrected macroscopic ensemble standard deviation computed from the unbiased variance estimator:

\[\sqrt{\left\langle \mathrm{Var}_{m,\text{unbiased}}(x_m)\right\rangle}.\]

Ensemble skill defined as RMSE of the ensemble mean.

Note

Skill is defined as the RMSE of the ensemble mean:

\[\text{Skill} = \sqrt{\left\langle (\bar{x} - y)^2 \right\rangle},\]

where \(\langle \cdot \rangle\) denotes the spatial mean.

This metric reduces the squared error over spatial/temporal dimensions before taking the square root (macroscopic RMSE), as is commonly done in ensemble forecast evaluation (and in LoLA/paper appendices).

In the default spatial-reduction evaluation path, this is numerically equivalent to the deterministic RMSE metric applied to an ensemble prediction tensor, because RMSE first averages over the ensemble dimension and then computes RMSE.

class WinklerScore(alpha=0.1, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: BTSCMMetric

Winkler interval score for central prediction intervals.

For significance level \(\alpha \in (0, 1)\), this metric computes central \((1-\alpha)\) prediction intervals from ensemble quantiles and returns the per-point interval score

\[ \begin{align}\begin{aligned}W_\alpha = (u - l) + \frac{2}{\alpha}(l - y)\mathbf{1}(y < l) + \frac{2}{\alpha}(y - u)\mathbf{1}(y > u),\\where :math:`l` and :math:`u` are the lower/upper interval bounds.\\Shape conventions ----------------- - Input prediction tensor: y_pred has shape (B, T, S..., C, M) - Input truth tensor: y_true has shape (B, T, S..., C) - Quantiles are computed along ensemble dim M: - l (lower): ``q_{alpha/2}``, shape (B, T, S..., C) - u (upper): ``q_{1-alpha/2}``, shape (B, T, S..., C) - y corresponds to y_true, same shape (B, T, S..., C).\\The internal ``_score`` returns pointwise Winkler scores with shape ``(B, T, S..., C)``. The base class then applies ``score_dims`` and ``reduce_all`` reductions: - ``score_dims='spatial'`` (default) -> ``(B, T, C)`` - ``score_dims='temporal'`` -> ``(B, S..., C)`` - ``score_dims=None`` -> ``(B, T, S..., C)`` - if ``reduce_all=True`` (default), ``compute()`` returns a scalar.\end{aligned}\end{align} \]

Lower values are better: narrow intervals are rewarded, and misses are penalized in proportion to their distance outside the interval.

Gneiting, T., & Raftery, A. E. (2007). Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation. Journal of the American Statistical Association, 102(477), 359-378. https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437

class MultiWinkler(coverage_levels=None, score_dims='spatial', dist_sync_on_step=False)[source]#

Average Winkler interval score across multiple central coverage levels.

This is the interval-score analogue of MultiCoverage: it evaluates a grid of nominal central prediction intervals and returns one scalar by averaging the Winkler score across interval levels, time, space, channels, and samples. Lower values are better.

Note

This is an unweighted average of central interval scores across the chosen coverage grid. The weighted interval score (WIS) uses a related multi-level interval-score construction with prescribed weights.
