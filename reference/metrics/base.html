rows_per_pair_block(elements_per_row, max_elements=134217728)[source]#

Leading-axis rows whose all-pairs block fits within max_elements.

All-pairs metrics cost O(D^2) per row and are independent across the leading axis, so an oversized input is processed in row blocks rather than refused: scoring a whole test split in one call costs extra passes instead of extra memory, and the result is unchanged.

Returns at least 1 – a single row is the indivisible unit, so a cap below one row’s cost degrades to row-by-row rather than raising.

Parameters:

  • elements_per_row (int)

  • max_elements (int)

block_pairwise_frames(y_pred_vector, y_true_vector, elements_per_row, max_elements, compute)[source]#

Apply an all-pairs compute frame-by-frame, in memory-capped blocks.

y_pred_vector is (*leading, D, M) and y_true_vector is (*leading, D), the output of a metric’s vector_dims selection (autocast.metrics.ensemble._vectorize_selected_dims()). Depending on vector_dims, leading can carry batch alone, or batch together with lead-time and/or channel – but every entry along it is an independent scoring frame: the all-pairs work for one frame never touches another. Blocking is therefore exact, not an approximation – flattening every leading axis into one blockable row axis (rather than only the batch axis) lets the row cap from rows_per_pair_block() reach below a single original batch row, e.g. also splitting across lead-time and channel for a spatial vector.

Parameters:

  • y_pred_vector (Tensor) – Ensemble predictions, (*leading, D, M).

  • y_true_vector (Tensor) – Ground truth, (*leading, D).

  • elements_per_row (int) – Element count of compute’s dominant intermediate for a single frame (e.g. D * D * M for an all-pairs (D, D, M) tensor, D * D for a (D, D) covariance).

  • max_elements (int) – Cap on one block’s total element count.

  • compute (Callable[[Tensor, Tensor], Tensor]) – Maps one row-block of (pred, true) – shapes (rows, D, M) and (rows, D) – to its per-frame result, (rows, ...).

Returns:

compute’s per-frame results, concatenated and reshaped back onto the original leading axes: (*leading, ...).

Return type:

Tensor

class BaseMetric(reduce_all=True, dist_sync_on_step=False)[source]#

Bases: Metric, Generic[TPred, TTrue], ABC

Shared template for spatial metrics that reduce over spatial axes.

⛔ The accumulator is RAGGED along the lead axis, and it has to be. InvalidFrameAction.TRUNCATE feeds each trajectory its valid prefix (InvalidFrameAction), so consecutive update calls carry different numbers of lead steps. A short call accumulates into the LEADING SLICE of the buffer and a long one GROWS it; lead_samples records, per lead step, how many trajectories actually reached that far, and compute() divides per lead rather than by one global count.

⛔ The per-lead count is not bookkeeping – it is the survivorship guard (D101). Once trajectories truncate at different frames a long-lead average runs over a shrinking, non-random subset, so a model looks BETTER at long leads precisely because its worst cases dropped out. The count is a metric state, reduced across ranks with the score, and it is what the evaluator publishes beside every windowed number.

⚠ Before this existed, self.sum_score += score_summed failed TWO ways on a ragged prefix, measured 2026-08-26 on jobs 6140907/6140908: a length-3 score into a length-4 buffer RAISED, and – worse – a length-1 score into a length-4 buffer BROADCAST silently, writing one lead’s value into all four.

update(y_pred, y_true)[source]#

Update metric state with a batch of predictions and targets.

A call may carry FEWER lead steps than an earlier one (a truncated prefix) or MORE (a full batch after a truncated one). Both are handled: the score lands on the leading score.shape[1] lead steps of the buffer, and the buffer grows when a call is wider than it is.

compute()[source]#

Compute final metric value.

⭐ Per-lead division, and it reduces to the old one exactly. Each lead step is divided by the number of trajectories that actually reached it, so the leads a truncated run never got to do not dilute the ones it did. When nothing truncates every entry of lead_samples equals total_samples and this is bit-for-bit the previous sum_score / total_samples.

⭐ ``reduce_all`` averages the LEADS EQUALLY, not by population, and the choice is deliberate. A population-weighted headline would shift weight onto the early leads exactly as a model truncates more, so diverging would IMPROVE its headline – a second flattering mechanism stacked on top of the thinned-subset one. Equal lead weight keeps two models’ headlines composed of the same leads however differently they truncate. It does NOT make the scalar survivorship-safe: a long lead averaged over three survivors still counts for as much as a short one averaged over three hundred, which is why lead_samples is published beside the number and why a headline is quoted only over the full-population horizon (D101).

Return type:

Tensor

align_lead_state(spec)[source]#

Grow (or materialise) the accumulator to spec, keeping its value.

⛔ A DDP requirement, not a tidy-up. dist_reduce_fx="sum" routes every state through torchmetrics’ _sync_dist, which gathers the ranks’ tensors and then calls torch.stack on them (torchmetrics/metric.py:533). gather_all_tensors pads to the largest size and trims each entry back to its OWN size (torchmetrics/utilities/distributed.py:138-150), so the stack sees differently-shaped tensors and RAISES. Under TRUNCATE the widths are a per-rank fact – one rank’s trajectories may all die at lead 3 while another’s run to 4 – so the widths have to be agreed before compute(). A rank that never updated is the same hazard in its sharpest form: its default state is 0-dimensional, which sends it down gather_all_tensors’ scalar branch while its peers all-gather sizes, and mismatched collectives hang rather than fail.

Parameters:

spec (LeadStateSpec) – The world’s agreed accumulator shape and dtype.

Raises:

ValueError – If spec is narrower than this rank’s own state, or disagrees with it away from the lead axis – either means the agreement was computed wrongly, and silently reshaping would sum two different quantities together.

Return type:

None
